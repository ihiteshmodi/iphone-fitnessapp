## Your current mix
Per ~90 min lifting session in an air-conditioned gym, with little sweat: 2 g Himalayan salt + 1 g potassium chloride in 750–1000 mL.
- Sodium ≈ 760 mg (≈ 505 mg/h)
- Potassium ≈ 524 mg

## What the notes say (Vol 05, hydration chapter)
| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Lifting 45–60 min | Water plus electrolytes is enough; no intra-workout carbs needed |
| Heavy sweating | Add about 300–700 mg sodium per hour (or per 500–1000 mL of fluid); at least ~500 mg/h when sweating heavily. Pick within the range by sweat and heat; learn sweat rate from a weigh-in before and after |
| Long or hot sessions | Per 500–750 mL: 500–700 mg sodium, 80–150 mg potassium, some carbs. Sodium matters most |
| Ordinary days | Drink to thirst; pale yellow urine is the aim |
| Urine | Dark day after day → drink more. Clear and frequent, again and again → may be drinking more than needed or losing minerals |
| Caution | Don't add salt on your own with high blood pressure or kidney disease |

## Reading of your mix
- Sodium ≈ 505 mg/h sits inside the 300–700 mg/h heavy-sweat range, though your sessions are low-sweat. It is enough; nothing to add for lifting.
- Potassium is well above the 80–150 mg per 500–750 mL in-session figure. The notes give no daily potassium target or upper limit for this, so no change is suggested. Revisit with Blood Labs (serum potassium) and blood pressure.
